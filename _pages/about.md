I am a Ph.D. software engineer at Google. My work spans distributed systems, cloud computing, machine learning systems, and computational economics.

My research brings economic mechanisms—including dynamic pricing, token markets, and incentive design—and econometric causal inference to the design and evaluation of large-scale cloud and datacenter systems. At Google, I work on machine-learning systems for understanding local shopping intent.

## Research highlights

- **Reliable cloud services.** [Rajomon](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi25/presentation/xing), published at USENIX NSDI 2025, is a market-inspired decentralized overload-control system for latency-sensitive microservices. Under heavy load, it improves goodput by more than 45% and reduces latency by more than 78% over state-of-the-art baselines.
- **Sustainable datacenters.** [Carbon Responder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08589) coordinates demand response across heterogeneous datacenter workloads while accounting for performance, fairness, and decentralized team incentives.
- **Causal analysis for cloud scheduling.** [CAPES](https://cloudintelligenceworkshop.org/papers/aiops26-Xing.pdf), presented at AIOps 2026, uses regression discontinuity design to estimate how score-based scheduling affects server power and LLM-serving latency.

I received my Ph.D. in Computer and Information Science from the University of Pennsylvania in 2026. My dissertation, _The Datacenter as an Economy: Markets, Incentives, and Causal Inference for Resource Management_, was advised by [Benjamin C. Lee](https://www.seas.upenn.edu/~leebcc/). I previously earned an M.S. in Economics and Computation from Duke University and a Bachelor of Economics from Wuhan University.
